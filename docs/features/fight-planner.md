# Fight planner

The fight planner (`/planner`, behind the `planner` flag) plans a party's heals and mitigations for one pull of an Extreme, Savage or Ultimate fight, from that party's FFLogs report. The backend only reads FFLogs ([backend docs](/docs/backend/features/fight-planner.html)); the planning runs in the browser.

## Libraries

| Library                   | What it holds                                                                                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `planner/feature-planner` | The page: report and pull pickers, the per-player tabs, the plan's chart and rotation, edits, the XLS export |
| `planner/data-access`     | Stores and API services; the plan as played (`plan-as-played.ts`)                                            |
| `planner/scheduler`       | The scheduler itself, loaded only on demand, so it stays out of the page's bundle                            |

Each library's `README.md` describes its files in detail. The scheduler's is the reference for how a plan is made.

## How a pull is planned

The flow, in `pull-planner.ts`:

1. **Mechanics:** the pull's hits are grouped into boss mechanics: raidwides, tank busters and targeted hits (`mechanics.ts`). A hit far bigger than that attack usually does is a failed mechanic, taken as avoided. A hit the fight doesn't let kill can't take the last HP: one logged at no HP with the player alive, or an attack that takes everyone to 1 HP whatever their HP (TEA's Photon).
2. **Tools:**
   - each player's mitigations, shields and heals at their level, from the backend's action catalog (`tools.ts`, `heal-tools.ts`);
   - each player's heal power, read from the pull (`heal-power.ts`).
3. **Openers:** a replanned player starts with their opener (`openers.ts`), as The Balance (or Icy Veins) gives it.
4. **Mitigation:** the party's mitigations and shields are planned first, as if every hit landed at full HP (`mitigation-plan.ts`); a player's own cooldown waits for their biggest hit (by its size, not the HP they have then), so a tank keeps theirs for their tank busters. Galvanize and the Eukrasian barriers don't stack: on a player who already has one, another does nothing, whatever its size (none of 2,853 landed in the logs), so only the one up first counts. A GCD shield (Succor, Adloquium) that would take its healer below 30% MP within 30s is taken back when the cooldowns picked after it made it unneeded (the next hits too, which land on what it left), so one healer isn't drained by shields while the other has MP to spare (`mitigation-take-back.ts`). Then each tank's cooldowns go on the autos they take whenever they're ready, after what the mechanics need (`tank-autos.ts`): the short ones (The Blackest Night, Holy Sheltron, Heart of Corundum, Bloodwhetting, Oblation), The Blackest Night only where it breaks, and the bigger ones that work on physical hits (Rampart, Reprisal, Guardian, Bulwark, Damnation, Shadowed Vigil, Great Nebula, Camouflage), so a stretch of heavy autos isn't left without. A warrior's Nascent Flash, which shares Bloodwhetting's recast, never goes on the warrior themselves, and on a raidwide only where a hit is short of the margin, so Bloodwhetting keeps the warrior's own hits and autos.
5. **Heals:** then the healers' heals (`heal-plan.ts`). First, in time order, so that no hit takes anyone below the margin (the HP that counts as dangerously low). Then everyone is refilled to full before each of the pull's mechanics, as healers do, with what those heals leave: a refill only uses a cooldown whose recast and gauge fit around every use already planned, later ones too, and its MP must last for every later heal. A GCD heal refills only from a healer with MP to spare (at least 30% of their max, the fuller one preferred: `heal-refill.ts`), so one healer doesn't drain while the other sits full, and only before a heavy mechanic: one with a hit taking 40% of its target's max HP after mitigation, before shields (50% when it hits fewer than 4 players). Before lighter ones only oGCDs and the GCDs that cost no damage (the Afflatus heals, Pneuma) refill, and no oGCD spending an Aetherflow stack (Lustrate, Indomitability), which costs the Energy Drain it would have been: in 63 ultimate kills' logs, healers topped everyone up before the heavy hits and left them near 90% before the rest. Before a heavy raidwide (one on 4 players or more) they also go in shielded, so there the damage no barrier takes yet counts as missing too, which only a GCD shield's barrier (Succor, Eukrasian Prognosis, Aspected Helios...) makes up: a healer shields a full party for it, and nobody shields a player already shielded. So do the raidwides in a replanned astrologian's Neutral Sect (`raidwide-shields.ts`), whatever their size, as the logs' astrologians cast about 1.5 Helios Conjunctions in each: there only Neutral Sect's own barrier counts as the shield, since it stacks with the others, and a Helios Conjunction (an Aspected Helios below level 96) cast in a Neutral Sect already up leaves it too, also when a Horoscope goes with that Helios (one Helios sets off both). Zoe makes the shield of the Eukrasian heal after it 50% bigger too, as the logs' sages pair it (86% of their Zoes go before a Eukrasian Prognosis). The single-target shields (Celestial Intersection, Divine Benison, Taurochole, Haima, Aquaveil, Exaltation) go on a tank, as the logs' healers cast them, and are kept for the busters: on a raidwide, or on someone not a tank, only where a hit would otherwise leave the margin. In 3 kills' logs, 1 of their 45 such casts went on a DPS or healer before a heavy raidwide; the plan had 83 there before this rule. A pairing's barrier is weighed by what it saves at the hits it reaches, as a GCD shield's is. The GCD heals the guides keep for when a healer is out of MP or every better heal is down (Cure II, Medica, Benefic II, Helios, Diagnosis, Prognosis) come last, after Medica III, Helios Conjunction, Aspected Benefic, Regen and the Afflatus heals, and never refill. Cure and Benefic aren't planned at all once Cure II and Benefic II are known: they heal the same member less for the same GCD (the logs have 7 Cures and 1 Benefic in 63 kills). Physick isn't planned either: Adloquium and the Aetherflow heals do its job, and as a last resort the plan still cast it 4 times as often as the logs. A GCD heal with a barrier (Succor, Adloquium, Eukrasian Prognosis...) is weighed with the damage its barrier will take off the hits ahead, not just its heal, but only on players without such a barrier up already: so a shield before a hit beats a bigger heal where the barrier counts. Helios is the exception while its caster's Helios Conjunction regen is up: astrologians follow one with it rather than overwrite the regen (89% of the Helios casts in the logs), so there it counts as any GCD heal. Where that plan leaves a hit short or someone dead, the pull is planned again the old way (every heal refilling before every mechanic, the last-resort heals like any GCD heal), and the plan with fewer deaths, then fewer short hits, is kept; where the better one is still short, the pull is planned again without the shields just for the heavy raidwides (which can draw the healers' cooldowns away from a chain of heavy hits), then that way with every heal refilling everywhere, and again the better one is kept. Each of these is tried only while the one before it came out better (`plan-variants.ts`): on the bench's 18 slowest kills, the same plans in 23% less time. The next round's mitigations plan for the HP the needed heals give (`hpNeeded`), not the refills. Several rounds replan the mitigations around the lowest HP the heals left. The boss's auto-attacks count with the plan's mitigations and shields, not those up as played (`auto-damage.ts`), so a tank's cooldowns as played don't count on top of the plan's. A tank's autos, every 3s or so, are healed for as one stream: the heals for one can go in from the last mechanic or from their autos 10s before, not just in the moment since the auto before (or the other tank's).
6. **Resources:**
   - each healer's MP (`mp.ts`, Lucid Dreaming, Thin Air);
   - a Dark Knight's MP: The Blackest Night costs nothing in the end when it breaks, as Dark Arts makes the next Edge of Shadow free (`dark-arts.ts`: it breaks once its target takes a quarter of their max HP in its 7s, as played). One that won't break costs an Edge, so it's planned only where a hit needs it, never for spare margin. One on the autos needs the MP only as it goes up. The Dark Knight's tab counts their Edges of Shadow against the log: those as played the plan drops for The Blackest Night's MP, and those more than played its MP pays for (Dark Arts, MP it saves; `dark-knight-edges.ts`: each at the earliest moment it never runs their MP out later), and the difference;
   - gauges (Aetherflow, Addersgall with Rhizomata, the astrologian's cards). A sage uses Rhizomata once a spend leaves fewer than two stacks (`rhizomata.ts`); the heals are planned again with its stacks, and if that comes out worse, with only those a heavy mechanic follows within 15s (as the logs' sages use it), then with one in the 15s before each of the heaviest stretches (the big hits' damage over 30s, 90s apart), else none, their stacks and weave slots given back. Of those kept, only the ones a spend needs stay: one whose stack no heal needs (every spend still fits the gauge without it) is dropped, as the logs' sages use it for the heals only (141 in 40 kills, where keeping it wherever the gauge ran low gave 320);
   - each replanned player's burst windows.
7. **Strategies:** the whole plan is made several ways, and the one with the fewest deaths is kept (`strategies.ts`). The ways: the plain one, the party waiting for bigger hits, big heals first, tank-buster chains, healers favouring healing, and Macrocosmos on cooldown. The plain one comes first; the others are tried only while someone dies to a mechanic. Where nobody does but a hit is left short, only the party waiting and the healers favouring healing are tried, in that order, stopping at the first plan with no hit short: on the bench (63 kills) they took the hits left short from 26 to 11, where the others only moved deaths around.
8. **Death repair:** each death left is retried with one big cooldown held back so it's up for that hit; a replan with fewer deaths is kept (`death-repair.ts`).
9. **Deaths that remain** are modelled, with a raise planned for each (`plan-deaths.ts`).

**Wipes:** a pull that wiped is completed with the mechanics the party never reached, from the fight's script (`completion-*.ts`). Players are planned from the wipe point on.

**Workers:** planning runs in web workers (`plan.worker.ts`, `plan-pool.service.ts`), so the page stays usable while it plans. A bar under the plan button shows how far it has got: each worker reports its plan's share done as it goes (`plan-progress.ts`: the first pass fills 60% of it, the passes tried after it the rest), and the bar shows the plans being made together, the plan with the user's edits and the one without, or the other ways tried while it looks for a better plan. The page shows the plain plan first, then looks for a better one by the same rules as above (`plan-refine.ts`, sharing `strategiesAfter` and `worthImproving` with `strategies.ts`): the other ways are made side by side, one worker each, and weighed in order, stopping once nobody dies to a mechanic and no hit is short; where someone still dies, a worker then repairs the best way's plan (`repairDeaths` in the plan's options), each replan its own part of the bar.

## Planning rules

- Always plan with the latest patch's values. Reports from an earlier expansion aren't planned.
- Prevent deaths first. If a death can't be prevented, model it and plan a raise.
- Hits past a wipe's end were never played, so they never count as failed.
- Every oGCD sits under a GCD in the rotation view.
- Openers come from The Balance, or from Icy Veins when The Balance's guide is older.
