# Fight planner

The fight planner (`/planner`, behind the `planner` flag) plans a party's heals and mitigations for one pull of an Extreme, Savage or Ultimate fight, from that party's FFLogs report. The backend only reads FFLogs ([backend docs](/docs/backend/features/fight-planner.html)); the planning runs in the browser.

## Libraries

| Library                   | What it holds                                                                                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `planner/feature-planner` | The page: report and pull pickers, the per-player tabs, the plan's chart and rotation, edits, the XLS export |
| `planner/data-access`     | Stores and API services; the plan as played (`plan-as-played.ts`)                                            |
| `planner/scheduler`       | The scheduler itself, loaded only on demand, so it stays out of the page's bundle                            |

Each library's `README.md` describes its files in detail. The scheduler's is the reference for how a plan is made.

## How a pull is planned

The flow, in `pull-planner.ts`:

1. **Mechanics:** the pull's hits are grouped into boss mechanics: raidwides, tank busters and targeted hits (`mechanics.ts`). A hit far bigger than that attack usually does is a failed mechanic, taken as avoided. Where the pull can't tell (an attack on two players or fewer that killed one who isn't a tank, with more than their max HP and no invulnerability up: `doubtfulAttacks`), a kill asks other groups' kills what that attack does, with the same request as for an attack blocked every time, so up to three more pulls are read for those kills alone (4 of the bench's 88). One none of them has a hit of does nothing done right, so that hit is a failed mechanic: an M11S samurai late to Assault Evolved's safe spot took 19 times their max HP from it, the attack's only hit in the pull, and the plan had stacked the party's cooldowns on it. Tanks are left out: their busters run to tens of times their HP and are taken under an invulnerability, or can't kill. A hit the fight doesn't let kill can't take the last HP: one logged at no HP with the player alive, or an attack that takes everyone to 1 HP whatever their HP (TEA's Photon).
2. **Tools:**
   - each player's mitigations, shields and heals at their level, from the backend's action catalog (`tools.ts`, `heal-tools.ts`);
   - each player's heal power, read from the pull (`heal-power.ts`).
3. **Openers:** a replanned player starts with their opener (`openers.ts`), as The Balance (or Icy Veins) gives it. A scholar's Aetherflow stacks (3 at each Aetherflow and Dissipation, none at the pull) go to the plan's heals first: the opener's Energy Drains, and those played after it, stay only where the final plan's heals leave a stack (before, the plan could show 4 stacks spent from one refill). In the opener, their oGCD attacks give way only to a step of the same action or one in their weave slot, so a paladin's Imperator, which their Confiteor needs, stays. A white mage's Afflatus Misery is the plan's own (three lilies after the last): theirs as played show as their usual attack, as does a sage's Eukrasian Dosis whose Eukrasia's slot a planned GCD took. A dancer's Improvised Finish shows "Improvisation, then", as a sage's Eukrasian heals show their Eukrasia.
4. **Mitigation:** the party's mitigations and shields are planned first, as if every hit landed at full HP (`mitigation-plan.ts`). What needs an enemy to use it on (Reprisal, Feint, Addle, Dismantle) waits out the time nothing can be targeted (`untargetable.ts`): the pull's downtime, but for a window where players cast at an enemy all the same, an add FFLogs has no targetability change for (TEA's Living Liquid). The plan had put them there 23 times on 10 kills, the logs never. Where each player is alone in their own instance (Necron's solo phase: each fights an add of their own while the boss is gone), what they use reaches only themselves (`solo-phases.ts`): no heal, shield, buff or mitigation of theirs lands on anyone else, and one meant for a single player can only be on them. The phase is read from the log, so a new fight has it before any fight script does: a stretch of 20s or more, the boss untargetable, in which nobody's heal or buff reaches anyone else, yet players take enemy hits (5 or more) and the healers heal themselves (twice or more); it ends at the next heal or buff on someone else. On the bench it finds both Necron EX kills' (about 251–303s) and nothing else: the other long stretches with nothing on others, cutscenes and transitions, have one self-heal at most. Planned that way, a sage there no longer spends Ixochole, Zoe and Eukrasian Prognosis II on a party they can't reach. Before anything else, each heavy tank buster (60% of the tank's max HP or more before mitigation) gets a short and a long cooldown on the tank it hits (`tank-busters.ts`), as The Balance's guides pair them ("Rampart and Camoflauge tied with Heart of Corundum, or Nebula tied with Heart of Corundum"): the short one is The Blackest Night, Holy Sheltron, Heart of Corundum or Bloodwhetting (the other tank's Intervention or Nascent Flash counts), the long one Rampart (a gunbreaker's with Camouflage), the 40% one (Guardian, Damnation, Shadowed Vigil, Great Nebula) or a warrior's Thrill of Battle; the weaker ones (Oblation, Dark Mind, Bulwark) are left for hits that need more. The most dangerous busters go first, and none where an invulnerability takes the hit; on a hit both still leave killing, they're taken back where one of the tank's invulnerabilities fits for it, and kept where none does (their Living Dead used just before). In 88 kills' logs, busters of 60% or more had a long cooldown or an invulnerability on the tank 86-92% of the time, those under 40% 28%; planned the old way, a tank's cooldowns went to a bigger hit first and left the next buster bare (a Dancing Mad gunbreaker took Chaotic Flare with only Sacred Soil). On the bench, busters with both went from 675 to 1,137 (the logs' tanks: 1,313), unsafe hits from 4 to 2, with no deaths added, and the healers' single-target shields moved toward the logs' counts (Celestial Intersection 371 to 332, logs 301; Exaltation 149 to 123, logs 120). After that, a player's own cooldown waits for their biggest hit (by its size, not the HP they have then), so a tank keeps theirs for their tank busters. Galvanize and the Eukrasian barriers don't stack: on a player who already has one, another does nothing, whatever its size (none of 2,853 landed in the logs), so only the one up first counts. A GCD shield (Succor, Adloquium) that would take its healer below 30% MP within 30s is taken back when the cooldowns picked after it made it unneeded (the next hits too, which land on what it left), so one healer isn't drained by shields while the other has MP to spare (`mitigation-take-back.ts`). Then each tank's cooldowns go on the autos they take whenever they're ready, after what the mechanics need (`tank-autos.ts`): the short ones (The Blackest Night, Holy Sheltron, Heart of Corundum, Bloodwhetting, Oblation), The Blackest Night only where it breaks, and the bigger ones that work on physical hits (Rampart, Reprisal, Guardian, Bulwark, Damnation, Shadowed Vigil, Great Nebula, Camouflage), so a stretch of heavy autos isn't left without. A warrior's Nascent Flash, which shares Bloodwhetting's recast, never goes on the warrior themselves, and on a raidwide only where a hit is short of the margin, so Bloodwhetting keeps the warrior's own hits and autos. Their heals that come with what the player does next are the plan's too, where it places the skill (`self-heals.ts`): Bloodwhetting's, Raw Intuition's and Nascent Flash's 400 potency with each of the warrior's weaponskills while it lasts (their GCDs as played), Tengentsu's Foresight's regen from the first hit on the samurai during Tengentsu, Damnation's Primeval Impulse from the first hit or as it ends, and Great Nebula's and Thrill of Battle's heal by the max HP they add; their healing as played is left out.
5. **Heals:** then the healers' heals (`heal-plan.ts`). First, in time order, so that no hit takes anyone below the margin (the HP that counts as dangerously low). Then everyone is refilled to full before each of the pull's mechanics, as healers do, with what those heals leave: a refill only uses a cooldown whose recast and gauge fit around every use already planned, later ones too, and its MP must last for every later heal. A spell that needs another up first (a scholar's Accession and Manifestation need Seraphism) is checked for that before its MP, so the plan counts as short of MP only where a spell it could have cast was refused for MP. A GCD heal refills only from a healer with MP to spare (at least 30% of their max, the fuller one preferred: `heal-refill.ts`), so one healer doesn't drain while the other sits full, and only before a heavy mechanic: one with a hit taking 40% of its target's max HP after mitigation, before shields (50% when it hits fewer than 4 players). Before lighter ones only oGCDs and the GCDs that cost no damage (the Afflatus heals, Pneuma) refill, and no oGCD spending an Aetherflow stack (Lustrate, Indomitability), which costs the Energy Drain it would have been; Lustrate refills never, kept for a hit that needs it (in 54 kills' logs, 23 on a tank to the plan's 113, now 28): in 63 ultimate kills' logs, healers topped everyone up before the heavy hits and left them near 90% before the rest. Before every raidwide (one on 4 players or more), light or heavy, they also go in shielded, as the logs' healers shield 60-67% of an ultimate's raidwides with a GCD shield whatever their size (and are refilled with GCD heals then), so there the damage no barrier takes yet counts as missing too, which only a GCD shield's barrier (Succor, Eukrasian Prognosis, Aspected Helios...) makes up: a healer shields a full party for it, and nobody shields a player already shielded by a healer's GCD shield (Galvanize, a Eukrasian barrier). Other barriers (Divine Veil, Shake It Off, Panhaima, Holos, Tempera Grassa) don't count, as the logs' sages put up Eukrasian Prognosis over them on nearly every heavy raidwide: on the bench the plan's Eukrasian Prognoses went from 1,576 to 2,029 (the logs' sages casting 2,084). So do the raidwides in a replanned astrologian's Neutral Sect (`raidwide-shields.ts`), whatever their size, as the logs' astrologians cast about 1.5 Helios Conjunctions in each: there only Neutral Sect's own barrier counts as the shield, since it stacks with the others, and a Helios Conjunction (an Aspected Helios below level 96) cast in a Neutral Sect already up leaves it too, also when a Horoscope goes with that Helios (one Helios sets off both). A replanned astrologian's Horoscope only ever goes before such a Helios, which makes it Horoscope Helios (400 potency, read again as the Helios lands or right after the next hit on those it reached, whichever heals more, so it doesn't heal a full party before a raidwide; shown as "Read again" in the plan), never on its own: the logs' astrologians upgrade 88% of their Horoscopes (51 of 55 with Neutral Sect up), and The Balance allows either, but the plan had cast 43% alone (`NEVER` in `heal-tools.ts`). Zoe makes the shield of the Eukrasian heal after it 50% bigger too, as the logs' sages pair it (86% of their Zoes go before a Eukrasian Prognosis): the mitigation step weighs each Eukrasian shield with Zoe before it too, wherever Zoe is back, and keeps it where the bigger barrier saves more (the plan's Zoes went from 74 to 279 on the bench, the logs' sages casting 238), but not on the plan made the old way, the fallback. A heal also counts what it makes up at the hits in the 30s after, at half weight, and a party heal in full: one heal on everyone that also covers the next hits is what healers cast instead of one on each member. When refilling, a heal on one member (Essential Dignity, Tetragrammaton, Druochole...) goes in only before a heavy mechanic, on a tank, or on someone else missing at least a fifth of their max HP; once three or more such players are missing HP, only on the tanks. Party heals top up the rest. In the logs, two thirds of the heals on one DPS went in with them missing 30% or more, and 22 of 177 under 10%; refilling everyone to full, the plan had put 416 of its 738 on a DPS missing under 10%. A heal a hit needs still goes on whoever needs it. The single-target shields (Celestial Intersection, Divine Benison, Taurochole, Haima, Aquaveil, Exaltation, and an astrologian's the Spire) go on a tank, as the logs' healers cast them, and are kept for the busters: on any other hit (a raidwide, or a hit on a few such as FRU's Path of Light), or on someone not a tank, only where a hit would otherwise leave the margin. In 3 kills' logs, 1 of their 45 such casts went on a DPS or healer before a heavy raidwide (the plan had 83); in 54, 45 went on a tank for a hit on a few (the plan had 656). The logs' astrologians put 93 of 129 Spires on a tank, where the plan had 66 of 279 (now 129 of 188). Where a plan made this way leaves someone dead or a hit short, the plan made the old way (shields for the heavy raidwides only, the tank shields kept only from raidwides, the Spire on anyone) is tried too. A pairing's barrier is weighed by what it saves at the hits it reaches, as a GCD shield's is. The GCD heals the guides keep for when a healer is out of MP or every better heal is down (Cure II, Medica, Benefic II, Helios, Diagnosis, Prognosis) come last, after Medica III, Helios Conjunction, Aspected Benefic, Regen and the Afflatus heals, and never refill. A tank's own oGCD heals (Equilibrium, Aurora) are planned like a healer's free oGCDs, in place of those played, as players use them a lot (the logs have about 6.5 Equilibriums a warrior and 7 Auroras a gunbreaker each kill); Thrill of Battle stays a healing buff as played. An astrologian's the Arrow (healing received +10% for 15s) goes on the tank taking the biggest hit in the next minute, just before it, and the heals after count it (`tank-buffs.ts`): the logs' astrologians put 83 of 110 Arrows on a tank, and the plan had only kept them as played. Busy with their damage too, astrologians play it from only about half their cards (114 of 223 Astral Draws in 88 kills' logs, little more often before heavy tank damage), so the plan plays it only where the tank takes at least 1.5 times their max HP of the mechanics' damage (unmitigated) in its 15s, and only before the next Umbral Draw, which takes it away (on 8 ultimate kills: 33 Arrows, logs 30). The Ewer (a 200-potency regen for 15s) goes the same way: one a draw, on the tank taking at least 0.8 times their max HP in its 15s, its healing in the plan's HP; the logs play it from 145 of 239 Umbral Draws, 126 on a tank, and the plan, playing it only for a short hit, had 65 (now, on 8 ultimate kills, 45 as the logs). A scholar's Protraction goes the same way whenever it's back, but last, with Earthly Star and the other aimed heals, in the weave slots the heals leave: placed before the heals, it took the slots a Dancing Mad scholar short of them needed (two deaths). Its boost isn't counted there. On the bench (88 kills): Arrows 0 to 273 (logs 117: players skip about half), Protractions 0 to 81 (logs 153), no deaths added. A DPS's Second Wind comes after all of them, and never refills either: DPS press it to save themselves where a healer would be too late, not to heal for the healers. Cure and Benefic aren't planned at all once Cure II and Benefic II are known: they heal the same member less for the same GCD (the logs have 7 Cures and 1 Benefic in 63 kills). Physick isn't planned either: Adloquium and the Aetherflow heals do its job, and as a last resort the plan still cast it 4 times as often as the logs. A GCD heal with a barrier (Succor, Adloquium, Eukrasian Prognosis...) is weighed with the damage its barrier will take off the hits ahead, not just its heal, but only on players without such a barrier up already: so a shield before a hit beats a bigger heal where the barrier counts. Helios is the exception while its caster's Helios Conjunction regen is up: astrologians follow one with it rather than overwrite the regen (89% of the Helios casts in the logs), so there it counts as any GCD heal. Where that plan leaves a hit short or someone dead, the pull is planned again with the shields only for the heavy raidwides, then the old way (every heal refilling before every mechanic, the last-resort heals like any GCD heal), and the plan with fewer players dying (to any damage, a damage-over-time tick too, `deadIn`), then fewer short hits, is kept; where the better one is still short, the pull is planned again without the shields just for the heavy raidwides (which can draw the healers' cooldowns away from a chain of heavy hits), then that way with every heal refilling everywhere, and again the better one is kept. Each of these is tried only while the one before it came out better (`plan-variants.ts`): on the bench's 18 slowest kills, the same plans in 23% less time. A hit is projected only with the uses from the 2 minutes before it (`coverage.ts`): no effect lasts longer, and the bench's 88 kills plan the same in 26% less time (Dancing Mad's 13 in 3,770s instead of 5,092s). Last, once the plan is picked, its heals are planned once more with an astrologian's Earthly Star and Macrocosmos and a sage's Pneuma whenever they're back, timed for the heaviest raidwide in the next minute (Earthly Star placed at least 10s before it, to be grown, and set off with Stellar Detonation 1s after it, or going off by itself where no weave slot is free: in the logs, 216 of 302 detonations landed in the 10s after a raidwide, 67 before it; it used to go off just before, on a party still full, Macrocosmos and Pneuma, damage spells, cast just before it in a free GCD; Macrocosmos set off with Microcosmos once the hits it gathers are in, also where it would heal as much at its end: a second after the last raidwide of its window, if that comes 4s before its end or sooner, as the logs' astrologians press it (37 of 92, a median 1.4s after a raidwide; the plan, on 8 ultimate kills, 18 of 34, the logs 16 of 37); else, where it heals on its own as its 15s end, shown then as an event that takes no slot: "Macrocosmos heals as it ends"), as players use them, and that is kept unless it comes out worse (more players dead, to any damage, or more hits short). Left out of every pass before (Lucid Dreaming's too), their healing can't lead the rounds to plan fewer shields. The next round's mitigations plan for the HP the needed heals give (`hpNeeded`), not the refills. Several rounds replan the mitigations around the lowest HP the heals left. Planned in time order, the heals spend a cooldown on the first need they meet, even one a harder hit later in its recast needed. So where the rounds still leave hits short, the heals are planned again with those hits' mechanics first, every tool open to them, and the rest around them: the recast, gauge and MP checks see every use already planned, later ones too, so the earlier mechanics can no longer take what those need. Where that leaves other hits short, it's done once more with those added. Each is kept only if it comes out better (no more players dying, then fewer or smaller short hits: `hard-first.ts`). The mitigations go first for those mechanics too, and an earlier plan's mitigation whose recast would still be running at one of them (and doesn't cover it) is placed again rather than kept (`freedForHard`), but for one that granted another still kept (`withGranters`): let go, a Neutral Sect placed again elsewhere left its Sun Sign behind, a Temperance its Divine Caress, a Seraphism its Accessions, 21 of them on 32 kills (letting both go instead, a Dancing Mad kill planned twice as long, with a hit short), so a big cooldown spent on a lighter hit just before can go to the heavy stretch instead, as the logs' healers keep Neutral Sect, Expedient and Shadowed Vigil for DM's Forsaken (the bench's hits under the margin went from 4 to 1). On the bench (88 kills): unsafe hits 13 to 10, kills short of MP 5 to 3, no deaths added, and planning in 35% less time, as fewer plans fall back to the other ways of planning them (`plan-variants.ts`). Their mitigations are planned first too, before the more dangerous mechanics': where a bigger hit took a tank's cooldowns first, the buster after it was left bare (a Dancing Mad gunbreaker's Chaotic Flare); with that, unsafe hits went from 2 to 1 and kills short of MP from 1 to 0 (the one left, M11S's Assault Evolved, killed a samurai late to the mechanic in the log too). The boss's auto-attacks count with the plan's mitigations and shields, not those up as played (`auto-damage.ts`), so a tank's cooldowns as played don't count on top of the plan's. A tank's autos, every 3s or so, are healed for as one stream: the heals for one can go in from the last mechanic or from their autos 10s before, not just in the moment since the auto before (or the other tank's).
6. **Resources:**
   - each healer's MP (`mp.ts`, Lucid Dreaming, Thin Air). A replanned player's played casts of what the plan places itself count for nothing: their MP is given back, their healing is left out of the HP (the healing pairings' too, Horoscope Helios and Synastry's bond, `pairedHealing`, and heals the log names otherwise, Haimatinon, `LOGGED_AS`), and the plan column doesn't show them, also where the log has them under another action of the same name (Horoscope read again, Pneuma's heal). Only their damage spells stay as played; where a planned GCD takes one's slot, that spell's MP is given back too (`mp-replaced.ts`). The plan places a healer's Lucid Dreaming where its heals run their MP low, then plans the heals again; from then on every MP check counts the plan's Lucids (`lucidCast`, the model's `fixed` uses) in place of those as played;
   - a Dark Knight's MP: The Blackest Night costs nothing in the end when it breaks, as Dark Arts makes the next Edge of Shadow free (`dark-arts.ts`: it breaks once its target takes a quarter of their max HP in its 7s, as played). One that won't break costs an Edge, so it's planned only where a hit needs it, never for spare margin. One on the autos needs the MP only as it goes up. The Dark Knight's tab counts their Edges of Shadow against the log: those as played the plan drops for The Blackest Night's MP, and those more than played its MP pays for (Dark Arts, MP it saves; `dark-knight-edges.ts`: each at the earliest moment it never runs their MP out later), and the difference; the MP line spends those, as real Dark Knights spend spare MP on damage (the bench's planned DRK MP averaged 6,045 without them, 5,166 with them, against 5,103 in the logs), while an edit still sees the MP before them (`spare`);
   - gauges (Aetherflow, Addersgall with Rhizomata, the astrologian's cards). A sage uses Rhizomata once a spend leaves fewer than two stacks (`rhizomata.ts`); the heals are planned again with its stacks, and if that comes out worse, with only those a heavy mechanic follows within 15s (as the logs' sages use it), then with one in the 15s before each of the heaviest stretches (the big hits' damage over 30s, 90s apart), else none, their stacks and weave slots given back. Of those kept, only the ones a spend needs stay: one whose stack no heal needs (every spend still fits the gauge without it) is dropped, as the logs' sages use it for the heals only (141 in 40 kills, where keeping it wherever the gauge ran low gave 320);
   - each replanned player's burst windows, lined up with the party's as played (`burst-windows.ts`): a window is where two players' raid buffs come within 15s (one player's where only they have any). In 88 kills' logs, 64% of windows came 120±3s after the one before, the rest later for downtime and hard mechanics (150s, 171s, 225s), so a two-minute buff (Divination, Chain Stratagem, Presence of Mind, Bloodfest, Lightspeed) waits for the next window once back, however late, and a shorter one (Fight or Flight, Inner Release, Delirium, No Mercy) for a window due within half its recast, else goes on cooldown. Every job's burst goes first, before the heals and mitigations (`burst.ts`), as players keep it in its window and heal around it (placed after, a scholar's Chain Stratagem slid 20s and more past its window and missed the next). What a buff enables goes with it, last, as players don't hold a heal for it (`burst-follow-ups.ts`), each as its tooltip says: Chain Stratagem's Baneful Impaction, Divination's Oracle, Presence of Mind's three Glare IVs, Fight or Flight's Goring Blade, No Mercy's Sonic Break, Bloodfest's Reign of Beasts combo, Delirium's three (Scarlet Delirium, Comeuppance, Torcleaver, or Impalement), Inner Release's Primal Rend and Primal Ruination, Infuriate's Inner Chaos. After each of the plan's buffs, theirs as played where it's in time (and the plan has no GCD of its own in its slot), else in the first free slot: a GCD only in place of one of theirs nothing later needs (one that grants something, gives gauge, a Cartridge or MP, or has a combo bonus stays: in the place of a Gnashing Fang, one left its Jugular Rip without what it needs). A played one the plan's buffs don't enable shows as their usual attack ("in place of"). Kept where played, on 32 kills about 800 of them came with no buff to enable them (157 Scarlet Deliriums with no Delirium, 140 Goring Blades with no Fight or Flight); now none, and as many as the logs' but for the Delirium combo (170 Torcleavers to 219: Delirium's 15s are mostly Syphon Strikes and Souleaters, which give the MP and gauge others need): on 12 kills, 60% of the plan's two-minute buffs land in a window (logs 64%, 26% before). An astrologian's go first, with their draws and damage cards as The Balance (7.3, 7.4) and the logs' astrologians play them (the Balance in a Divination window 83% of the time, the Spear 90%): Astral Draw when its recast is back (the odd minute), the Balance and Lord of Crowns held to the next Divination, then Umbral Draw and the Spear in that window. Each draw swaps the cards in hand: after Astral Draw the Balance, the Arrow, the Spire and Lord of Crowns; after Umbral Draw the Spear, the Bole, the Ewer and Lady of Crowns. So the cards the heals play come at the draw as placed and go at the other draw (`redrawn`, `empties` in a card's pool): Lord of Crowns is never played after an Umbral Draw, nor Lady of Crowns after an Astral Draw (on the bench, 98 planned Ladies had no Umbral Draw of their own before).
7. **Strategies:** the whole plan is made several ways, and the one with the fewest deaths is kept (`strategies.ts`). The ways: the plain one, the party waiting for bigger hits, big heals first, tank-buster chains, healers favouring healing, and Macrocosmos on cooldown. The plain one comes first; the others are tried only while someone dies to a mechanic. Where nobody does but a hit is left short, only the party waiting and the healers favouring healing are tried, in that order, stopping at the first plan with no hit short: on the bench (63 kills) they took the hits left short from 26 to 11, where the others only moved deaths around.
8. **Death repair:** each death left is retried with one big cooldown held back so it's up for that hit; a replan with fewer deaths is kept (`death-repair.ts`).
9. **Deaths that remain** are modelled, with a raise planned for each (`plan-deaths.ts`).

**Wipes:** a pull that wiped is completed with the mechanics the party never reached, from the fight's script (`completion-*.ts`). Players are planned from the wipe point on.

**Workers:** planning runs in web workers (`plan.worker.ts`, `plan-pool.service.ts`), so the page stays usable while it plans. A bar under the plan button shows how far it has got: each worker reports its plan's share done as it goes (`plan-progress.ts`: the first pass fills 60% of it, the passes tried after it the rest), and the bar shows the plans being made together, the plan with the user's edits and the one without, or the other ways tried while it looks for a better plan. The plain plan itself is made with its fallback ways side by side (`plan-side-by-side.ts`): the plain way and each fallback (`variantsOf` in `plan-variants.ts`, each asked for alone with `oneWay`) start at once, one worker each, and are weighed in order by the planner's own rules (`triesNext`, `keepsNext`), the ways no longer needed stopped (`plan-pool.service.ts` drops a plan whose signal aborts). It is the same plan the worker would make trying them in turn, sooner where the plain way falls short (on the bench, DM's first plan in about half the time). The page shows the plain plan first, then looks for a better one by the same rules as above (`plan-refine.ts`, sharing `strategiesAfter` and `worthImproving` with `strategies.ts`): the other ways are made side by side, one worker each, and weighed in order, stopping once nobody dies to a mechanic and no hit is short; where someone still dies, a worker then repairs the best way's plan (`repairDeaths` in the plan's options), each replan its own part of the bar.

## Planning rules

- Always plan with the latest patch's values. Reports from an earlier expansion aren't planned.
- Prevent deaths first. If a death can't be prevented, model it and plan a raise.
- Hits past a wipe's end were never played, so they never count as failed.
- A Dark Knight under Living Dead survives once healed by their max HP in all before Walking Dead ends, as in the game (Undead Rebirth), whatever hits them meanwhile (`judged-hits.ts`); a hit that leaves them HP sets off no Walking Dead, so healing them back to full is enough. Judged by their HP when Walking Dead ended, 6 of the bench's 10 short hits were tanks the plan had healed out of it.
- Every oGCD sits under a GCD in the rotation view.
- Openers come from The Balance, or from Icy Veins when The Balance's guide is older.
